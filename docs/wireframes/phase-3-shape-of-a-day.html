Ariadne wireframes · phase 3 of 4

The shape
of a day

Seven items are started at once and Today is empty. This phase adds the moment that asks you to choose, the arithmetic that tells you the truth about how much you chose, and a freshness readout you can actually trust. It adds no calendar, no suggestion, and no clock position — ever.

01/P8 · trust before features

The header stops making a claim it can't keep

"Last synced: just now" is a single global sentence over two independent providers. If the Azure DevOps token expired an hour ago, the dashboard still says just now — on a manual-refresh product that is the most damaging failure available, because completeness is the entire value.

  1. 1Two readouts, two truths. No global "synced" sentence can be true while a source is failing, so the global sentence is deleted rather than qualified.
  2. 2Rows from the failed source carry their own age. Stale data is never mistaken for current data; the dashboard is never blanked because one provider is down.
  3. 3The banner names the cause and the remedy and does not apologise. Auth, rate limit and network each get their own sentence, and each is tested.
  4. 4Refresh becomes a ghost button beside the status it acts on. It is maintenance, not the job.
  5. 5Sprint progress gains a bar with two marks — done, and elapsed — so "0/17 with 13 days left" reads as the signal it is. Text stays for screen readers, and the progressbar role carries aria-valuenow.
  6. 6One ambient number: 9 waiting on you. Its own denominator, its own group, no relationship to 0/17.

02/P7 · the empty state becomes the door

Today, before you've chosen

Currently the most valuable card on the page spends itself on one sentence and a text link that reads like it might rearrange your afternoon. Replace it with a real button and one fact.

The gold rule down the left edge is the only place brand colour enters the product. It marks Today, and Today only — which is also the thread this document is drawn on.

03/P7 · four steps, no recommendation

Plan the day

Sunsama's ritual with the calendar surgically removed. Every step is a decision you make; nothing is ordered, suggested, or timeboxed for you. Re-enterable at any hour without losing state.

Copy discipline "Your call." is the entire product philosophy in two words: state the arithmetic, hand back the decision. It must never become "consider dropping one" or "this looks like a lot". If a future diff softens that line into advice, the diff is the bug.
Decision · default capacity §12 question 5 asks whether to prompt for capacity on first run. No — pre-fill 6h and make it editable right here, in the step where the number means something. A first-run question about your daily capacity is a question with no context behind it; the same question next to "8h 30m planned" answers itself.

04/P7 · P9 · after the ritual

Today, once you've chosen

A hand-ordered flat list. Per-item estimate, per-item logged time, plan total against capacity. No time-of-day field exists — not in the schema, not in the UI, not ever.

Guardrail check Today and In progress stay disjoint — the member-picker item shows In Progress because that is its upstream ADO state, and it sits in Today because you picked it. The local status that drives the In progress section is unchanged and still exclusive.

05/P7 · P9 · closing

Wrap up — mostly already built

ShutdownDialog.tsx already does the hard part: completed today, still open, total hours logged, carry to tomorrow. What it lacks is the plan it was measured against, an estimate-vs-actual line, and the free-text note. It also lacks a name that says what it does — it is currently reached from a link called "Review my day", sitting in the Today header where a planning control belongs.

Framing, which is this task's real risk "Review-type work ran 40% over estimate again" is calibration — second person, about a kind of work, derived from your own logs, computed locally. It becomes self-surveillance the moment it turns into a rate, a streak, a grade, or a comparison. There are none of those anywhere in this phase, and the copy review is part of the definition of done, not a step afterwards.

The two diffs to reject on sight (a) "Suggested for today." However gentle, that is an algorithm deciding — INV-3 and INV-4 both. (b) A time-of-day field on a plan item. That is the calendar arriving through the side door; the first version is always framed as a convenience. Both start small. Neither gets in. Nothing in this phase decides when you do anything, and no order on screen is machine-chosen except the score, which shows its arithmetic.
